This one is a dead horse, but let's beat it for the sake of completeness.

---

Like every historical event, we're talking about a collection of threads, rather than a linear story. In the left-wing narrative, GamerGate is generally taken to be the origin of all of the world's current ills, such as the organizational aspects of modern decentralized hate movements: the selection of targets, the separation between useful lackeys and hardliners, along with an overbearing narrative that unites the two in their organic activity within the web. This is - in great part - absolutely true. GamerGate is particularly impenetrable in that it affected so many areas of political life in such specific ways, that a "thorough recollection" of it requires employing a large amount of technical and cultural knowledge - many of the most important feuds that took place as a result of it and in "provocation" to it are small, and would otherwise be historically insignificant. Part of the reason it has been able to preserve its own history is its effective (unintentional) exploitation of the divisions of labor.

We do not have the collective knowledge to rival this beast, but we can bring up some specifics. Enough time has passed that it'd probably be a good thing to question parts of the progressive narrative while conclusively affirming that GG was a hate movement, almost as a kind of favour.

Even in places outside the US and Europe, where this was not exactly true, a similar script started being written as Internet access rose and living standards decreased; similarities are not coincidences.

ex-boyfriend of the Depression Quest developer Zoe Quinn accuses them of cheating and having sex with a games journalist in exchange for a review (In reality, this review never existed and what was actually posted was a cursory mention that probably had nothing to do with sex); both the guy and Zoe were not-that-good to each other and their relationship was in a state of uncertainty, but the guy takes the cake with being awful due to trying to bring an online mob to harass her. 

This post eventually reaches various online communities after being deleted from a few forums and given its own site. The outrage is generally broad, and at this point it is misogynistic, but what actually organizes the outrage are not public forums. The people who really stick to it are those who saw the narrative trickle down to their IRC chats on servers like Freenode. They're the real "psychos", who ride along and guide the moral panic that has, at this point, fully merged with the hate mob for Anita Sarkeesian, the other favorite misogynistic hate target on the internet. It turns into the general whining about le politics in le games.

It all blows up at the tail-end of August 2014: On the 24th of August, Sarkeesian posts a new video, on the 27th of August, an actor called Adam Baldwin cites the controversy on Twitter and divulges its name. Most importantly, on the 28th of August 2014, a journalist at the Game Developer Magazine writes an article called "Gamers are over", which is a quite contemptuous little piece that is clearly the result of them being pissed at the harassment being faced both in journalistic and game development circles. 

The article, while not really remembered, is the most important part of it all, as there is a reason for its appearance in GDM, a game industry business, art, and programming magazine. At this time, GamerGate's real origin within IRC culture and the groups which mass-produced narratives is well-known in technical circles, though the sentiment around it uncertain. To fully dissect the different types of resentment and grievances which GG-like discourse actually voiced in circles like TIGSource would take a whole study which I can't write and which no one wants to write. 

Generally, within the dev scene, GG was a vehicle for the old guard of indie development which did not see mainstream success to bring light to their problems with people like Zoe Quinn and Phil Fish, who were, to a certain extent, very literate in playing ball with the games media and were actually corrupt in their involvement with indie scene in North America; Zoe made off with crowdfunding money, Fish got double-award for in a category in IGF where accumulating them was vetoed, etc. This, notably, does not mean that these grievances meant anything to a wider audience; Fish was still targeted mainly because he was a leftist, and the harassment directed towards Quinn was clearly misogynistic.
