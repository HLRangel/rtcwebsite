From the Center

Conclusions/Notes on the Meeting Round, 11-12 of November

After our conversation on the 12th, I have regained faith in our capability to work towards something meaningful, especially in the next year. Although the effective body of people who can do any meaningful work remains small, it’s at the very least existent, which is more than you can say for a lot of other grouplets out there.

After consulting with others, I have decided to not call for any sort of software jam, as it would be a draining endeavor and we wouldn’t be able to guarantee a meaningful quorum.

An outreach project that we will start to work on soon is an annotated version of Principles of Communism to serve as an introductory text, aiming for wide distribution. Something our movement solely lacks is an introductory body of work that can connect with a great amount of people while not omitting important information or shaving away parts of the communist program in order to conform to one of its many revisions. Of course, we have to keep in mind the fact of censorship laws which today see themselves strengthened by both the bourgeois left and right, and work around them, in order to make our writing at the very least not criminal to possess in most countries.

In relation to the archival goals of TBLOC, I have decided that we should not persue making a Chrome extension, simply because the APIs we’d use to do so are awfully designed.

We’ll work on a web app to do page archival from a form. Since writing something like this fully in JavaScript seems to me like it would at the very least be a pain in the ass, this seems like a good moment to start development on our WebAssembly toolchain. Specific tasks will be specified later, after I’ve made inroads into developing a memory allocator in C (programs compiled to WASM have access to no standard library). If, after that, anyone wants to pitch in with another language, they’re welcome to do so, as WASM makes it extremely easy to interoperate. Either way, the end goal is the following: Create an app that can archive a page while preserving most static content - text, stylesheets, and images.

I have established a Signal chat for the sharing of important messages, seeing as we have had no success in establishing a mailing list (no one checks e-mail nowadays, after all). There’s a channel in our discord chat specifically dedicated to sharing Signal usernames now.

We had a long and fruitful discussion about the current state of the movement. I have personally re-emphasized the notion that to prescribe forms to organizations is useless, and we should avoid doing so when talking about operational security. In our conversation, we have also talked about how passive surveillance today takes center-stage, and the way in which it is universalized makes it so individual attitudes are, by themselves, quite meaningless. Today, no organization can succeed in fighting direct surveillance by a state actor, and so we should not focus on developing these counter-measures while we do not even know the methods ourselves. The important thing is fighting against the everyday ways in which the movement is compromised.

I have called out the incredibly stupid position of self-martyrdom held by a certain trotskyist organization, which I view as the prime example of the kind of group that must first face the threat of decomposition by consequence of their operational attitude before even considering change in earnest. I have postulated that a group “being made an example of” has no positive consequence for the wider movement, mainly because there are no lessons a group which bases its action on fundamentally flawed conceptions can take. The role we must take is an active one, directed towards creating the educational basis for a new consciousness that will arise in the future, from the forms created by the militants themselves in interaction with one-another.

A question remains open, in the same way it has remained for the past three years: how will we do this?

Of course, now we have the benefit of clarity in what not to do, which is an amount of insight greater than that which is held by most. We now understand the real difference between democracy as form and principle, and we now understand why outreach directed towards existing discontent in technical spaces is completely useless, because it departs from the democratic principle. No longer will we waste our time in devising the security model of the future, and our intention is no longer to be a subservient provider. Our role may not be exclusive, but it is a real attempt to prefigurate, to develop a position before it can really translate itself in doctrine.

The call for anyone who wants to take advantage of our free static web hosting in order to host a political site is still open.
